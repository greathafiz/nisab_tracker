# Nisab gram weights: 85 g / 595 g vs 87.48 g / 612.36 g

Research date: 24 Oct 2026. Status: research notes only. No code has been changed.

## Summary and recommendation

- Both pairs of figures convert the **same** classical Nisab: 20 mithqal (dinar) of gold and 200 dirhams of silver. They differ only in the gram weight assumed for one mithqal: **4.25 g** (from weighing surviving Umayyad dinars) or **4.374 g** (from the Hanafi/South Asian barley-grain and tola system). Neither is a different ruling. The gap is about 2.9%.
- **85 g / 595 g** is the more widely published figure. It is used by AAOIFI Shari'ah Standard 35, Qaradawi's _Fiqh al-Zakah_, Jordan's Iftaa', Egypt's Dar al-Ifta, the UAE Fatwa Council, Ibn Uthaymin, Indonesia (BAZNAS and its ministry regulation), Malaysia, Zakat Foundation of America, NZF Canada and Islamic Relief Australia.
- **87.48 g / 612.36 g** (7.5 tola / 52.5 tola) is the Hanafi South Asian standard. It is used by Deoband, Banuri Town (in tola), the Hanafi darul iftas in the UK and South Africa, NZF UK, Islamic Relief Worldwide and Canada, and Muslim Aid UK.
- Other figures exist: Ibn Baz uses 92 g gold / 644 g silver, Turkey's Diyanet uses 80.18 g / 561.2 g, and Australia's ANIC uses 85 g of 21-carat gold / 609 g silver.
- The site owner heard right: the major standard-setting and fatwa bodies outside South Asia mostly use 85 g / 595 g. The site's own blog says 87.48 g "is used by most major Islamic charitable organizations", but the evidence doesn't support that claim.
- **Recommendation:** make the standard a setting, with a default that depends on the country. Pakistan, India and Bangladesh default to **87.48 g / 612.36 g (7.5 / 52.5 tola)**. Every other country defaults to **85 g / 595 g**. Let users switch on the calculator and the country pages, and add a one-line explanation (draft wording in section 4). If only one global figure is possible, use **85 g / 595 g** and keep 87.48 g / 612.36 g as the "Hanafi / tola" option.
- Fix the code inconsistency regardless (section 5). Mahr al-Fatimah uses the 2.975 g dirham while Diyyah and the silver Nisab use the 4.374 g basis. The blog posts also mix the two standards.

## 1. Classical basis

### Hadith

- **Sunan Abi Dawud 1573** (from Ali ibn Abi Talib, graded _Sahih_ by al-Albani on sunnah.com): "When you possess two hundred dirhams and one year passes on them, five dirhams are payable. Nothing is incumbent on you, that is, on gold, till it reaches twenty dinars. When you possess twenty dinars and one year passes on them, half a dinar is payable…" Arabic: «فَإِذَا كَانَتْ لَكَ مِائَتَا دِرْهَمٍ … حَتَّى يَكُونَ لَكَ عِشْرُونَ دِينَارًا». Verified at https://sunnah.com/abudawud:1573
- **Sahih al-Bukhari 1447 and 1459** (from Abu Sa'id al-Khudri): "no Zakat is imposed on less than five Awaq of silver" («وَلَيْسَ فِيمَا دُونَ خَمْسِ أَوَاقٍ مِنَ الْوَرِقِ صَدَقَةٌ»). Verified at https://sunnah.com/bukhari:1459 and https://sunnah.com/bukhari:1447. One uqiyah is 40 dirhams, so 5 awaq is 200 dirhams. Ibn Abd al-Barr records consensus on this (_al-Istidhkar_ 3/127, quoted by Dorar, below).
- **Ratio:** 10 dirhams weigh 7 mithqal, so 1 dirham = 0.7 mithqal. Ibn Khaldun, Abu Ubayd, al-Nawawi and others report consensus on this. Qaradawi quotes Ibn Khaldun (_Fiqh al-Zakah_ I, p. 129), and Dorar quotes the others (https://dorar.net/feqhia/2153). So **the silver Nisab is always 140 mithqal**, and every gram figure follows from the mithqal weight alone.

### The 4.25 g figure (primary source, verified)

Qaradawi, _Fiqh al-Zakah_ Vol. I (English translation by Monzer Kahf, King Abdulaziz University, pp. 130–131; PDF at https://www.islamicstudies.info/literature/fiqhalzakah_vol1.pdf):

> "Fortunately, several dinars that were minted at the time of 'Abd al Malik have been preserved. Some European scholars and the Egyptian 'Ali Basha Mubarak, in separate studies, take the weight of several Islamic dinars that exist in London, Paris, Madrid, and Berlin. They come to the conclusion that the dinar of 'Abd al Malik weighed 4.25 grams. … Thus the dirham would be 4.25 x 7/10 = 2.975 grams. … It seems that the survey of preserved coins is the most accurate means of determining the exact weight of the dinar and the dirham. The nisab of silver must then be 200 x 2.975 = 595 grams, and the nisab of gold should be 20 x 4.25 = 85 grams of gold."

### The 4.374 g / tola figure

- Mufti Ebrahim Salejee (South Africa) gives the derivation: "1 grain of barley is equivalent to 43, 74 mg", the shar'i dirham is 70 grains, "70 x 43, 74 mg = 3.0618 grams", and "200 x 3.0618 … = 612.36 grams". Source: https://muftionline.co.za/node/521 (verified).
- In the tola system, 20 mithqal = 7.5 tola and 200 dirhams = 52.5 tola. Banuri Town's fatwa 144607102858 (29-01-2025) says: «آج کل یہ بیس دینار وزن کے اعتبار سے ساڑھے سات تولہ بنتےہیں … دوسو دراہم … ساڑھے باون تولہ چاندی» ("these twenty dinars by weight come to 7.5 tola … the 200 dirhams … to 52.5 tola of silver"). Source: https://www.banuri.edu.pk/readquestion/ahadees-ki-roshni-mein-sona-chandi-ke-nisaab-ke-dalaail-aur-maujooda-zamane-mein-sona-chandi-ki-qeemat-mein-tafawut-ke-paish-e-nazar-asal-nisaab-e-zakat-ki-tahqiq-144607102858/29-01-2025
- With 1 tola = 11.664 g: 7.5 × 11.664 = 87.48 g and 52.5 × 11.664 = 612.36 g. The site's `TOLA_GRAMS = 11.6638` (in `lib/countries.ts`) gives 87.479 g and 612.35 g, which matches within rounding.
- Caveat: NZF UK says a Pakistani tola is 12.5 g in jewellery trade usage (https://nzf.org.uk/knowledge/how-do-you-convert-your-gold-from-bhoris-tolas-and-voris-into-grams/). The fiqh tola is 11.66 g. The site's "per tola" price uses 11.6638 g, which is the correct fiqh tola.

## 3. Is either treated as "more correct"?

- **Arguments for 85 g / 595 g.** Qaradawi calls the survey of preserved coins "the most accurate means" (p. 131). Dorar's fiqh encyclopedia (reviewed under the Saudi Fiqh Society methodology) argues the same, saying the coin method «أمثل الطرق … وأبعدها عن الخطأ» (it is the best method and the least prone to error). It adds that although these weights are slightly lower than the barley-grain method, «إلا أنه أحوط في باب الزكاة، وأرعى لمصلحة الفقراء» (it is more cautious for zakat and better serves the poor). The barley-grain results also vary between scholars. https://dorar.net/feqhia/2153 **V**
- **Arguments for 87.48 g / 612.36 g.** Hanafi muftis treat the tola figures as the established conversion in the madhab's books (Askimam cites _al-Hidaya_, _al-Bahr al-Ra'iq_ and _Fatawa Mahmudiyya_). Deoband and Banuri use them as settled practice. This is a madhab-internal standard, not a claim that the coin measurements are wrong.
- **Bodies that acknowledge both.** NZF UK and Islamic Relief Australia each present one pair and call the other "another opinion". A Darul Uloom Trinidad answer reportedly says the difference "is not a major one, and all can be used" (search excerpt only, **S**). Ibn Baz considered both 85 g and 92 g and chose 92 g, while saying that paying at 85 g as a precaution is good.
- **The site's current blog wording is not supported.** It says 87.48 g is "more precise" and used by "most major Islamic charitable organizations". The 4.374 g figure is not more precise than the 4.25 g figure; they come from different methods. Charity practice is split.
- **Practical difference.** 87.48 / 85 = 1.0292 and 612.36 / 595 = 1.0292. Both thresholds are about **2.9% higher** under the tola standard. In money terms that is small. The gold-vs-silver choice is far bigger (gold Nisab ≈ 7–10× the silver Nisab at recent prices). Using the lower 85 g / 595 g figures means slightly more people owe zakat, which matches the "cautious for the poor" reasoning.
- **Gold vs silver (briefly).** This is a separate question. Hanafi bodies, Islamic Relief, islamqa.info (https://islamqa.info/en/answers/370380) and ANIC favour the silver Nisab for cash. Qaradawi, ZFA, NZF Canada, the UAE Council and Malaysia/Indonesia use gold.
- **Purity and pricing differences matter more than 85 vs 87.48 in some countries.** Egypt applies 85 g to 21-carat gold and ANIC uses 21-carat. BAZNAS reportedly prices income-zakat gold at 14-carat (secondary source). Malaysia uses the previous year's average price. The site uses pure-gold spot prices everywhere, so it won't match these countries' official numbers exactly either way.

## 4. Recommendation for nisabtracker.com

**Options considered**

1. _Keep 87.48 g / 612.36 g everywhere._ Correct for Hanafi South Asia and the UK charity sector. But it contradicts the official figures in Indonesia, Malaysia, Egypt, Jordan, the UAE, AAOIFI and US/Canada bodies. A Malaysian or Indonesian user will see a Nisab about 2.9% above their zakat board's figure.
2. _Switch to 85 g / 595 g everywhere._ Matches AAOIFI and most fatwa bodies. It is the lower, more cautious threshold. But it breaks the tola FAQ for Pakistan, India and Bangladesh (85 g is not 7.5 tola) and differs from Deoband, Banuri and NZF UK.
3. _Per-country default plus a user toggle (recommended)._
   - Add a `standard` setting: `"standard"` = 85 g / 595 g (dinar 4.25 g), and `"hanafi-tola"` = 87.48 g / 612.36 g (7.5 / 52.5 tola).
   - **Default `hanafi-tola` for Pakistan, India and Bangladesh** (the `usesTola` countries). Keep the tola FAQ there.
   - **Default `standard` for the other 17 countries**, Nigeria included. Nigeria is predominantly Maliki, and Maliki and other non-Hanafi sources use 85 g / 595 g. No Nigerian body I could find publishes gram figures.
   - The UK is the hardest case, because NZF UK, Islamic Relief and Muslim Aid UK all use 87.48 g / 612.36 g. Defaulting the UK to `hanafi-tola` would also be defensible. I lean towards `standard` with a visible toggle, for consistency with AAOIFI. The owner should decide.
   - Show the grams actually used next to every value (the site already prints "612.36 grams of silver", so this just becomes dynamic). Remember the user's choice in localStorage.
4. _If a toggle is too much work:_ switch everything to 85 g / 595 g and add a note: "Hanafi (South Asian) scholars use 87.48 g / 612.36 g (7.5 / 52.5 tola), about 2.9% higher." On the three tola pages, either keep 87.48 g / 612.36 g or change the tola FAQ to say 85 g ≈ 7.29 tola. Mixing the two would be confusing.

**Suggested user-facing text (one or two sentences)**

> "Nisab is 20 dinars of gold or 200 dirhams of silver. Most scholars and AAOIFI convert this to 85 g of gold / 595 g of silver; Hanafi scholars in South Asia use 87.48 g / 612.36 g (7.5 / 52.5 tola), about 3% higher. Both are valid—choose the one your scholar follows."

Short tooltip version: "85 g / 595 g (AAOIFI standard) · 87.48 g / 612.36 g (Hanafi / tola)".

```ts
export const SILVER_NISAB_GRAMS = 612.36; // 200 × 3.0618 g
const MAHR_AL_FATIMAH_GRAMS = 1487.5; // 500 dirhams × 2.975g   <- 4.25 g basis
const DIYYAH_GOLD_GRAMS = 4374; // Approximately 1000 dinars   <- 4.374 g basis
```

The silver Nisab and Diyyah use the 4.374 g mithqal (dirham 3.0618 g). Mahr al-Fatimah uses the 2.975 g dirham, which belongs to the 85 g / 595 g standard. So the site's dowry is 500 × 2.975 g, while its own dirham elsewhere is 3.0618 g. Consistent values under each standard:

| Value                                | 85 g / 595 g standard (dinar 4.25 g, dirham 2.975 g) | 87.48 g / 612.36 g standard (dinar 4.374 g, dirham 3.0618 g) |
| ------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Gold Nisab (20 dinars)               | 85 g                                                 | 87.48 g                                                      |
| Silver Nisab (200 dirhams)           | 595 g                                                | 612.36 g                                                     |
| Mahr al-Fatimah (500 dirhams silver) | 1,487.5 g                                            | 1,530.9 g (= 131.25 tola)                                    |
| Diyyah (1,000 dinars gold)           | 4,250 g                                              | 4,374 g                                                      |

Suggested fix: derive all four from two base constants (`DINAR_GRAMS`, with `DIRHAM_GRAMS = DINAR_GRAMS * 0.7`), so they can't drift apart and switch together if a standard toggle is added. Dowry and Diyyah aren't displayed today, so this is latent. _Not verified here:_ the amount of Mahr al-Fatimah (500 dirhams is the common figure; some Hanafi sources give 480 dirhams) and the 1,000-dinar Diyyah. I only checked their arithmetic.

Minor: `TOLA_GRAMS = 11.6638` (`lib/countries.ts:13`) × 52.5 = 612.35 g, not 612.36 g. Harmless, but if `SILVER_NISAB_GRAMS` is ever derived from tola it will print 612.35.

- L62: "Some scholars round this down to 85 grams (using 4.25g per Mithqal). Inaccurate. 85 g is not a rounding; it is a separate measurement, and it is used by AAOIFI and most fatwa bodies. Charity usage is split.
